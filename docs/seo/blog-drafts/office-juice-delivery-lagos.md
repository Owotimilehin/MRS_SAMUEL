## Why offices are switching to fresh juice

Office drinks tend to fall into two camps: coffee and sugary soft drinks. Both have their place, but neither does much for an afternoon slump that hits around 3pm.

Cold-pressed juice offers a different option. Every Mrs. Samuel bottle is 100% fruit and vegetables, with no added sugar, no water and no preservatives. It's something staff can reach for that feels like a treat and is made from real produce.

It also says something about your workplace. A fridge stocked with fresh juice is a small, visible sign that a company cares about the people who work there.

## Office juice delivery in Lagos: how standing drops work

The simplest setup is a standing order. Instead of placing a fresh order every time the fridge runs low, you agree a regular drop with us:

- **Weekly drops** suit offices where juice is part of everyday life, such as a stocked staff fridge or a reception area that hosts visitors.
- **Monthly drops** suit teams that want juice for a monthly town hall, team day or end-of-month celebration.

Standing weekly or monthly drops are invoiced together, so your finance or admin team deals with one invoice instead of a pile of small receipts.

### Getting the quantity right

Because our juice has no preservatives, it should be kept refrigerated at 2–6°C and drunk within 4 days. That shapes how you plan a weekly drop:

1. **Count your regular drinkers, not your headcount.** Not everyone will take a bottle every day.
2. **Plan for the days people are actually in.** If your team works a hybrid week, plan around office days.
3. **Check your fridge space.** Make sure there's room for the full drop at the right temperature.
4. **Start modest and adjust.** It's easier to add bottles after a couple of weeks than to watch extras go past their best.

We bottle in 650ml, and some flavours also come in 330ml. Ask us which ones are available in the smaller size. Smaller bottles usually work best for a staff fridge, as one bottle is one serving.

## Supporting staff wellness

Many Lagos companies now run wellness weeks, health talks or step challenges. Fresh juice fits naturally into these, as long as it's part of a bigger picture rather than the whole programme.

Ideas that work well:

- **A wellness week fridge** stocked with lower-sugar green and vegetable juices.
- **A "swap your soft drink" challenge**, where staff trade one sugary drink a day for a fresh juice or water.
- **Hot-season hydration** during the hottest months, when the walk from the bus stop leaves everyone drained. Our post on [cold-pressed juice and the Lagos heat](/blog/cold-pressed-juices-beat-lagos-heat) has more on this.

We'd gently suggest keeping wellness messaging honest. Juice is a pleasant, nourishing drink, not a cure for anything. If you'd like something to share with staff about sugar and juice, our [honest guide to juice and weight loss](/blog/best-juice-for-weight-loss-nigeria) is a good starting point.
