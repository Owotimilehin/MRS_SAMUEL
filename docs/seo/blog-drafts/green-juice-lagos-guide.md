A few years ago, green juice was something you saw on foreign wellness pages, not in a Lagos fridge. Today, more people are asking for it by name. But if you've never tried one, the colour alone can be off-putting. Will it taste like grass? Is it only for gym people? This is our plain guide to green juice in Lagos: what goes into a bottle, what to expect when you take the first sip, and who it might suit.

## What is a green juice?

A green juice is a juice made mostly from green vegetables and leaves, usually with a little fruit for balance. Unlike a fruit punch, where sweetness leads, a green juice leans on fresh, watery vegetables like cucumber and celery, and leafy greens like spinach or kale.

The fruit, usually green apple, pineapple or lemon, is there to round off the flavour so it's pleasant to drink rather than bitter.

## Green juice in Lagos: the produce that works

The good news is that you don't need imported, hard-to-find ingredients to make a good green juice. Much of what goes into a green bottle is easy to find in Lagos markets.

### Cucumber

Cucumber is the backbone of most green juices. It's mostly water, so it gives a green juice its light, refreshing body. It's also widely available and affordable year-round in Lagos.

### Spinach and kale

Leafy greens give green juice its colour. Spinach is mild and soft in flavour. Kale is stronger and a little more "green" tasting. Both bring vitamins and minerals found in dark leafy vegetables, such as folate and vitamin K.

### Ugu (fluted pumpkin leaf)

Ugu is a Nigerian kitchen staple, better known in soups like egusi and edikang ikong. It's also a lovely leaf to juice. It has a deep, earthy flavour and is part of our Veggie Burst blend.

### Celery

Celery adds a slightly salty, savoury freshness. It's one of those flavours people either love or learn to love. A little goes a long way.

### Apple, lemon and ginger

These are the balancers. Apple adds gentle sweetness. Lemon brightens everything and cuts through any bitterness. Ginger gives a warm kick that many Nigerians already love from zobo and pineapple-ginger drinks.

## What does green juice taste like?

Let's be honest: a green juice won't taste like pineapple juice. If you go in expecting a sweet fruit drink, you may be surprised.

A well-made green juice should taste:

- **Fresh and light**, thanks to the cucumber
- **Slightly earthy or "green"**, from the spinach, kale or ugu
- **Lightly savoury**, from the celery
- **Gently sweet at the finish**, from the apple or pineapple

The first sip is often the strangest. Give it a few sips before you decide. Green juice is a taste many people grow into, and it's at its most refreshing when it's properly cold.

### Tips if you're new to green juice

1. **Drink it very cold.** Cold softens earthy flavours. Keep it in the fridge at 2–6°C.
2. **Shake the bottle well.** Natural juices settle, and shaking brings the flavour back together.
3. **Start with a lighter blend.** Lemon Sip or Pure Green are gentler than a heavy, leafy mix.
4. **Pair it with food.** A green juice alongside breakfast can feel easier than drinking it on its own.

## Who might green juice suit?

Green juice can be a good fit for:

- **People cutting back on sugary drinks.** A vegetable-led juice is a lower-sugar swap for soft drinks and sweet fruit punches.
- **People who struggle to eat enough vegetables.** It's not a replacement for eating vegetables, but it can be a pleasant extra.
- **Busy professionals** who want something fresh in the morning without cooking.
- **Anyone who prefers less sweet drinks.** Some people simply like the clean, savoury taste.

Studies suggest that diets rich in vegetables and leafy greens may support overall health. Juice is one way to enjoy them, but whole vegetables still bring the fibre that juicing removes.

### A note of caution

Green juice isn't for everyone in every situation. Leafy greens such as spinach and kale are rich in vitamin K, which can matter for people on blood-thinning medication. Spinach also contains oxalates, which some people are advised to limit. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or take regular medication, check with your doctor before adding green juice to your daily routine.

And to be clear: green juice is a nourishing drink, not a cure or treatment for anything. We'd be wary of anyone who sells it as a "detox" miracle.
