Every January, and again every time a wedding date is fixed, the same question lands in our WhatsApp: "Which one is the best juice for weight loss in Nigeria?" It's a fair question, and we'd rather give you an honest answer than sell you a miracle. So here it is, plainly: no juice will melt fat. But the right juice, used the right way, can be a genuinely useful swap in a Lagos diet that is often heavy on sugar.

Let's talk about what juice can and can't do, and how to choose well.

## Juice is not a magic weight-loss tool

Weight comes down to a lot of things at once: how much you eat overall, what you eat, how you sleep, how much you move, stress, hormones and your own medical history. A bottle of juice sits inside all of that. It doesn't cancel out a plate of fried rice and turkey, and it doesn't "burn" anything.

Be wary of anyone who tells you a juice will "flush out fat", "detox your belly" or make you lose a set number of kilos in a week. Those promises aren't backed by good evidence, and a juice-only diet can leave you hungry, tired and back where you started once you return to normal meals.

If you're trying to lose weight for health reasons, or you're managing something like diabetes or high blood pressure, please speak to your doctor or a registered dietitian first. They can look at your full picture in a way no blog post can.

## The sugar question

Here's the part many juice sellers would rather skip. Even 100% fruit juice with nothing added still contains natural sugar. When fruit is pressed, the sugar comes out with the juice. The World Health Organization counts the sugars in fruit juice as "free sugars", the same category it advises people to keep low across the day.

That doesn't make fruit juice bad. It means portion and choice matter. A sweet, fruit-heavy bottle such as pineapple or orange is a lovely treat, but if weight is your focus, it's better enjoyed in a smaller glass than downed by the litre.

## Why whole fruit still wins

Pressing separates juice from most of the insoluble fibre in the fruit. Fibre is what slows digestion, helps you feel full and keeps your blood sugar steadier. That's why eating an orange tends to fill you up more than drinking the juice of three.

So our honest advice: keep eating whole fruit. Pawpaw, watermelon, garden egg, cucumber, guava and oranges from the market are cheap, filling and full of fibre. Think of juice as something that sits alongside whole fruit and vegetables, not something that replaces them.

## Where juice can actually help: replacing soft drinks

This is where juice earns its place. Think about a typical Lagos day: a chilled soft drink at lunch, a malt drink in the afternoon, maybe a sweetened packaged "juice drink" in traffic. Those add up to a lot of sugar with very little nutrition alongside it.

Swapping some of those for a low-sugar, vegetable-led juice can help in two ways:

- **Less sugar overall.** A juice built mostly on cucumber, celery and leafy greens carries far less sugar than a soft drink or a sweet fruit punch.
- **Something real in the glass.** Instead of empty calories, you're getting the vitamins, minerals and plant compounds that come with fresh vegetables.

Studies suggest that cutting back on sugary drinks is one of the simpler changes that may support weight management over time. A green juice won't do the work for you, but it can make the swap more pleasant than plain water every single time.

## Best juice for weight loss in Nigeria: what to look for

When you're choosing a juice with weight in mind, look for these things:

1. **Vegetables first.** Cucumber, celery, spinach, kale and ugu should do most of the work, with fruit there for balance.
2. **No added sugar.** Check the label. Words like "syrup", "sucrose", "glucose" or "sweetened" are a sign to put it back.
3. **No added water or concentrate.** Diluted or reconstituted drinks often add sugar back to make up for lost flavour.
4. **A sensible portion.** A glass, not a whole bottle in one go, especially for fruit-heavy blends.

## Simple ways to use juice well

- **Swap, don't add.** Replace a soft drink with your juice, rather than having both.
- **Pair it with food.** A green juice alongside a protein-rich breakfast, such as eggs or beans, is more filling than juice alone.
- **Drink it cold and fresh.** Our juices are pressed in the morning and delivered cold. Keep them in the fridge at 2–6°C and finish within 48 hours.
