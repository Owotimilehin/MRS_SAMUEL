No juice cures a hangover. US health authorities say time is the only cure. But alcohol makes you lose fluid, so drinking water and a small glass of juice can help you feel more comfortable, and ginger may settle a queasy stomach. The best protection is drinking less alcohol in the first place.

## What causes a hangover?

The [US National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism (NIAAA)](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/hangovers) lists several things that likely combine to make you feel rough:

- **Mild dehydration.** Alcohol blocks a hormone that tells your kidneys to hold on to water, so you urinate more and lose fluid. This likely adds to thirst, tiredness and headache.
- **Disrupted sleep.** You may fall asleep faster after drinking, but your sleep is broken and you wake early.
- **Stomach irritation.** Alcohol irritates the lining of the stomach, which can cause nausea and pain.
- **Inflammation.** Alcohol increases inflammation in the body, which may add to that unwell feeling.
- **Acetaldehyde.** When your body breaks alcohol down, it makes a toxic by-product called acetaldehyde.
- **Mini-withdrawal.** As alcohol leaves your body, you may feel shaky, anxious or restless.

Notice that most of these have nothing to do with a lack of vitamins. That is why no drink fixes them all.

## Is there a hangover cure?

No. NIAAA states plainly that there is no cure for a hangover other than time, and that the only way to completely avoid one is to not drink, or to keep alcohol to a minimum. Coffee, a cold shower or more alcohol in the morning will not cure it.

A [systematic review in the BMJ](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16373736/) looked at trials of hangover remedies, including medicines, supplements and even fructose and glucose sugars. It concluded that no compelling evidence exists that any conventional or complementary remedy prevents or treats a hangover, and that the most effective approach is to avoid drinking too much.

So when a juice brand tells you its bottle "cures hangovers", they are not telling the truth. We won't.

## Where juice can actually help

Juice cannot speed up how fast your liver clears alcohol. But it can help you feel a bit more human while you wait.

**1. Fluids**

Because alcohol makes you lose fluid, rehydrating is sensible. Water is the best choice. A small glass of juice counts towards your fluid too, and some people find it easier to drink something with flavour when they feel sick.

**2. A gentle start for your stomach**

When you cannot face food, a small glass of fresh juice with a few crackers, bread or plain pap can be an easy first step. Take it slowly. On a very irritated stomach, acidic juices like orange or pineapple can make heartburn worse, so a milder juice may sit better.

**3. Ginger for nausea**

Ginger has a long history for an unsettled stomach. The [NIH's NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ginger) says research shows ginger may help with nausea and vomiting in pregnancy, while studies for other types of nausea, such as motion sickness, have been less positive. It has not been tested well for hangovers. Still, many people find a ginger juice soothing.

**4. Something better than another sugary drink**

A cold soft drink or energy drink feels tempting, but it adds sugar and sometimes more caffeine. A small glass of fresh juice with water is a kinder choice.

## Watermelon, coconut water and "electrolytes"

You will see watermelon and coconut water recommended for hangovers. Both are mostly water and contain some potassium. They are pleasant ways to rehydrate. There is no good evidence that they cure a hangover. If you have been vomiting a lot, an oral rehydration solution from a pharmacy is designed to replace lost salts, and you should speak to a doctor or pharmacist if you cannot keep fluids down.

## What to avoid the morning after

- **More alcohol.** "Hair of the dog" only delays the hangover. NIAAA notes a morning drink will not cure it.
- **Paracetamol after heavy drinking.** NIAAA warns that combining alcohol and acetaminophen (paracetamol) can be toxic to the liver. Pain relievers like aspirin and ibuprofen can irritate the stomach. Ask a pharmacist before taking anything.
- **Driving.** You may still have alcohol in your blood the next morning, and a foggy head slows your reactions. Do not get behind the wheel on Third Mainland Bridge feeling unwell.
- **Huge, greasy meals on an empty, irritated stomach.** Start small.

## When a hangover is more than a hangover

Get medical help straight away if someone who has been drinking shows any of the signs of alcohol overdose listed by [NIAAA](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/understanding-dangers-of-alcohol-overdose):

- Confusion, or they are hard to keep awake or cannot be woken
- Slow or irregular breathing
- Vomiting
- Seizures
- Clammy skin, a bluish skin colour or a very low body temperature

Alcohol overdose is an emergency. And if you often wake up with hangovers, or feel you need a drink to get going, talk to a doctor. Help is available and confidential.

## How to make the next party easier

The most reliable "hangover juice" is the one you drink at the party, between alcoholic drinks.

1. **Eat before you drink.** A proper meal slows how fast alcohol is absorbed.
2. **Alternate.** One glass of water, juice or a mocktail between each alcoholic drink.
3. **Set a limit** before you arrive, and count your drinks.
4. **Drink water before bed.**
5. **Plan your ride home** so nobody drives after drinking.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best juice for a hangover?

No juice cures a hangover. A small glass of a mild, ginger-based juice with water may help you rehydrate and settle a queasy stomach. Water is still the most important drink, and time is the only real cure.

### Does orange juice help a hangover?

It gives you fluid and vitamin C, but there is no evidence it speeds recovery. Orange juice is acidic, so on an irritated stomach it can cause heartburn. Try a small amount with some plain food.

### Is ginger good for hangover nausea?

Ginger may help some types of nausea, according to NCCIH, but it has not been well studied for hangovers. Many people find it soothing. If you are vomiting repeatedly and cannot keep fluids down, see a doctor.

### Is it safe to take paracetamol for a hangover?

Be careful. The NIAAA warns that combining alcohol with acetaminophen (paracetamol) can harm the liver. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor before taking pain relief after heavy drinking.

### How can I avoid a hangover?

Drink less, or not at all. Eat before drinking, alternate alcoholic drinks with water or juice, and drink water before bed. The NIAAA says the only sure way to avoid a hangover is to avoid alcohol or keep it to a minimum.
