Tigernut (aya in Hausa, ofio in Yoruba, aki Hausa in Igbo) is a small, sweet tuber, not a nut. It is rich in starch, fibre and healthy fats, mostly oleic acid, with minerals such as magnesium and potassium. Eaten raw, soaked, as flour or as kunu aya, it is filling. Many popular claims, especially about fertility, lack human evidence.

Every Lagos bus stop has someone selling small mounds of tigernut, often next to coconut and dates. It is one of the most loved snacks in the country, and also one of the most hyped. This guide covers what tigernut really is, what it brings to your diet, the claims to take with a pinch of salt, and practical ways to eat, drink and store it.

If you are here for the drink, read our dedicated post on [kunu aya (tigernut drink) benefits](/blog/tigernut-kunu-aya-benefits).

## What is tigernut?

Tigernut is the small tuber of a sedge plant, Cyperus esculentus. It grows underground, a bit like a tiny, wrinkled sweet potato. Despite the name, it is not a nut, so it is generally safe for people with tree nut allergies (though see the allergy note below).

It goes by many names:

- **Aya** in Hausa
- **Ofio** or imumu in Yoruba
- **Aki Hausa** in Igbo
- **Chufa** in Spain, where it is made into horchata de chufa
- **Earth almond** in some English texts

Much of Nigeria's tigernut is grown in the North, and Nigeria is one of the world's larger producers.

## Brown, yellow and black tigernut

In Nigerian markets you will usually find:

- **Yellow (light brown) tigernut.** Bigger, plumper, juicier and the most common fresh snack. Good for kunu aya.
- **Brown (darker) tigernut.** Smaller and a bit more fibrous. Often sold dried.
- **Black tigernut.** Less common, smaller and sweeter, sometimes priced higher.

Fresh tigernut is plump and crunchy. Dried tigernut is shrivelled and hard, and needs soaking before you eat or blend it.

## Tigernut nutrition

A 2022 review in the journal Foods by [Yu and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8871521/) describes tigernut as a wholesome crop containing starch, fats, protein, fibre, vitamins, minerals and bioactive compounds such as flavonoids. A few points stand out:

- **Fat.** Tigernut is surprisingly rich in fat, and that fat is mostly oleic acid, the same kind that dominates olive oil.
- **Fibre.** Whole tigernuts are high in fibre, much of it insoluble.
- **Starch, including resistant starch.** Resistant starch is not fully digested in the small intestine and feeds gut bacteria further down.
- **Minerals.** Including magnesium, potassium and iron.
- **Protein.** Lower than true nuts or groundnuts.

Exact numbers vary by variety, soil and whether the tuber is fresh or dried, so be wary of websites that quote one precise figure.

## 6 real benefits of tigernut

**1. A filling, fibre-rich snack**

Fibre slows digestion, keeps you regular and feeds gut bacteria, as the [Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/carbohydrates/fiber/) explains. A handful of tigernut keeps you fuller than a pack of biscuits.

**2. Food for your gut bacteria**

Tigernut's resistant starch and fibre reach the large intestine, where gut bacteria ferment them. A small pilot study in Spain, discussed in our [kunu aya post](/blog/tigernut-kunu-aya-benefits), found early signs that tigernut drink may encourage helpful bacteria. More research is needed.

**3. Good fats**

The oleic acid in tigernut is a monounsaturated fat. Swapping saturated fats for unsaturated ones is part of standard heart-healthy eating advice.

**4. Minerals for everyday body work**

Your body uses [magnesium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-Consumer/) for muscles, nerves and energy, and [potassium](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Potassium-Consumer/) helps your heart, muscles and nerves work normally (NIH Office of Dietary Supplements). Tigernut adds some of both.

**5. Naturally gluten-free and dairy-free**

Tigernut flour has no gluten, and tigernut milk has no lactose. That makes it useful for people who avoid either, though anyone with coeliac disease should check that the flour was not processed alongside wheat.

**6. Naturally sweet**

Tigernut tastes slightly sweet on its own, so you can use it in drinks and baking with less added sugar.

## Tigernut myths we do not repeat

You will see claims online that tigernut boosts sperm count, cures erectile problems, increases female fertility, treats diabetes or "flushes out" toxins. Solid human studies for these claims are lacking. Some come from animal studies; others from nowhere. If you are trying for a baby or managing a health condition, speak to a doctor.

## How to eat tigernut

**As a snack.** Wash fresh tigernuts well and eat them raw. Dried tigernuts need soaking first (see below). Many people pair them with coconut and dates.

**As kunu aya (tigernut milk).** Soak, blend with water, dates, coconut and ginger, then strain. Full steps are in our [kunu aya guide](/blog/tigernut-kunu-aya-benefits).

**As tigernut flour.** Dry soaked or fresh tigernuts fully in the sun or a low oven, grind finely and sieve. Use it in pancakes, chin chin, cookies or swallow blends. It is sweeter and heavier than wheat flour, so mix it with other flours at first.

**In smoothies and oats.** Leftover pulp from making tigernut milk adds fibre to smoothies, oats and baking. Use it the same day or freeze it.

**As tigernut oil.** Cold-pressed tigernut oil is sold in some health shops. It is rich in oleic acid and is used for cooking and skincare.

## How to soak dried tigernut

1. Pick out stones and bad tubers.
2. Wash several times until the water runs clear. Tigernut grows in soil and carries sand.
3. Cover with clean water and soak in the fridge for 24 to 48 hours, changing the water at least once.
4. Rinse again before eating or blending.

Soaking in the fridge, not on the counter, keeps them from fermenting in Lagos heat.

## Who should be careful

- **People with sensitive stomachs.** Lots of fibre at once can cause bloating and gas. Start with a small handful.
- **People with diabetes or watching calories.** Tigernut is energy-dense, and tigernut drinks often include dates. Keep portions moderate and ask your doctor.
- **People with allergies.** Tigernut allergy is rare, but it happens. Tigernut is often sold next to groundnuts and processed with other foods, so if you have a serious allergy, ask first.
- **Anyone buying street tigernut.** Wash it well. Tigernut is grown in soil and handled a lot before it reaches you.

## How to store tigernut in Lagos

- **Fresh tigernut:** refrigerate in a breathable bag and eat within a few days. It can mould quickly in humid heat.
- **Dried tigernut:** keep in an airtight container in a cool, dry cupboard. It lasts for months.
- **Soaked tigernut and tigernut milk:** fridge only, and use within a day or two.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is tigernut called in Nigeria?

Tigernut is called aya in Hausa, ofio or imumu in Yoruba, and aki Hausa in Igbo. In Spain it is chufa.

### Is tigernut a nut?

No. Tigernut is a small tuber, the underground part of a sedge plant called Cyperus esculentus. Allergy to it is rare, but anyone with a serious food allergy should still check how it was processed.

### What are the benefits of tigernut?

Tigernut is filling and rich in fibre, resistant starch, oleic acid (a healthy fat) and minerals such as magnesium and potassium. It is naturally gluten-free and dairy-free. Early research suggests it may support gut bacteria, but studies are small.

### Does tigernut increase sperm count or fertility?

There is no solid human evidence that it does. Most claims come from animal studies or social media. If you are trying for a baby, speak to a doctor.

### How much tigernut should I eat a day?

There is no official amount. A small handful as a snack is a sensible portion. Start small if you are not used to much fibre, as it can cause bloating.

### How do I make tigernut milk?

Soak washed tigernuts in the fridge for 24 to 48 hours, blend with clean water (plus dates, coconut and ginger if you like), strain through a clean cloth and chill. Drink within a day or two.
