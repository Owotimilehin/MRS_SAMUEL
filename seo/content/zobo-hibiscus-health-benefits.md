## What is zobo?

Zobo (from the Hausa **zoborodo**) is made from the calyces of the roselle plant, Hibiscus sabdariffa. The calyx is the fleshy red cup around the flower's seed pod. Farmers in the North harvest and dry them, and they travel south to every market in the country.

The same drink is loved far beyond Nigeria:

- **Bissap** in Senegal and much of West Africa
- **Sorrel** in Jamaica and the Caribbean, often with ginger at Christmas
- **Agua de jamaica** in Mexico
- **Karkade** in Egypt and Sudan

In Nigeria, zobo is usually boiled with ginger, cloves and sometimes pineapple peel or garlic, then sweetened. Some people add cucumber, orange or watermelon for a fruity version.

## What is in a glass of zobo?

The deep red colour comes from **anthocyanins**, the same family of pigments found in berries and red cabbage. In the lab they act as antioxidants. Hibiscus also contains plant acids, which give zobo its sour, mouth-watering taste, plus small amounts of vitamin C and minerals.

What ends up in your glass depends heavily on the recipe. Long boiling may reduce heat-sensitive vitamin C. The biggest variable is sugar: a home or roadside zobo can carry as much sugar as a soft drink.

## 5 benefits of zobo, with the honest evidence

**1. It may modestly lower blood pressure**

This is zobo's best-studied effect. In a [randomised, placebo-controlled trial published in the Journal of Nutrition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20018807/), adults with slightly raised blood pressure drank three cups of hibiscus tea a day for six weeks. Their systolic (top number) blood pressure fell more than in the placebo group, and people who started with higher readings saw a bigger change.

Keep this in proportion. The study was small, the change was modest, and it used plain hibiscus tea, not sweetened zobo. Zobo is not a substitute for blood pressure medicine. If you have high blood pressure, keep taking what your doctor prescribed and ask before adding hibiscus daily.

**2. It gives you plant antioxidants**

Anthocyanins and other polyphenols in hibiscus show antioxidant activity in lab studies. Diets rich in coloured fruit and vegetables are linked with good health, though that does not mean any single drink protects you from disease. Think of zobo as one more colourful plant in your week.

**3. It keeps you hydrated**

Zobo is mostly water, and it is usually drunk ice-cold. On a hot Lagos afternoon, a lightly sweetened zobo is an easy way to get fluid in. The [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/nutrition-and-healthy-eating/in-depth/water/art-20044256) notes that drinks other than water count towards your daily fluid. Water should still be your main drink.

**4. It can replace a soft drink**

The real health win is often what zobo replaces. A home-made zobo with little or no added sugar is a far better daily habit than a bottle of soda. The [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news/item/04-03-2015-who-calls-on-countries-to-reduce-sugars-intake-among-adults-and-children) advises adults and children to cut free sugars, so the less sugar in your zobo, the better.

**5. The ginger adds its own benefits**

Most Nigerian zobo includes fresh ginger. Ginger has decent research behind it for easing nausea, especially in pregnancy, according to the [NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ginger). ## Claims we do not make

Nigerian social media is full of big zobo claims: that it cures typhoid, "washes" the kidneys, treats diabetes or boosts fertility. There is no good human evidence for any of these. If you are ill, see a doctor. Zobo is a drink to enjoy, not a treatment.

## Who should be careful with zobo

- **People on blood pressure medicine.** Because hibiscus may lower blood pressure, adding a lot of it on top of medicine could push it too low. Talk to your doctor first.
- **People on other regular medicine.** Herbs can interact with drugs. Ask your doctor or pharmacist if you take anything daily.
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding women.** Little is known about the safety of large amounts of hibiscus in pregnancy. Ask your doctor or midwife before drinking it often.
- **People with diabetes.** The sugar is the issue, not the hibiscus. Unsweetened or lightly sweetened zobo in small glasses is the safer choice. Check with your doctor.
- **Anyone with sensitive teeth or reflux.** Zobo is acidic. Drink it with a meal and rinse your mouth with water after.

## How to make good zobo at home

This makes about two litres.

1. Rinse two handfuls of dried zobo leaves well in clean water to remove dust and sand.
2. Put them in a pot with two litres of clean water, a thumb of sliced fresh ginger and a few cloves.
3. Bring to the boil, then simmer for 10 to 15 minutes. You do not need an hour.
4. Turn off the heat and leave to steep until cool.
5. Strain through a clean sieve or cloth.
6. Sweeten lightly, if at all. Try blending in fresh pineapple or orange juice instead of sugar.
7. Bottle in clean containers and refrigerate straight away.

**Cold-brew option:** soak rinsed zobo leaves and ginger in clean cold water in the fridge overnight, then strain. The taste is softer and less bitter. Use water you would drink straight from the tap only if it is treated; otherwise use boiled-and-cooled or bottled water.

## Why roadside zobo sometimes upsets stomachs

Zobo with no preservatives spoils quickly in heat. Problems usually come from unclean water, unwashed containers, or bottles left out of the cold for hours. Buy from sellers who keep bottles in ice, avoid bottles that look fizzy or smell fermented, and refrigerate home-made zobo straight away. Drink it within a day or two.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the benefits of zobo drink?

Zobo gives you fluid, plant antioxidants called anthocyanins and a little vitamin C. Small trials suggest hibiscus tea may modestly lower blood pressure in some adults. With little or no sugar, it is also a far better daily drink than soda. It does not cure any disease.

### Does zobo lower blood pressure?

Some research suggests it may, modestly. A placebo-controlled trial found adults with slightly raised blood pressure who drank hibiscus tea daily for six weeks saw their systolic reading fall more than the placebo group. It is not a replacement for medicine, so talk to your doctor if you have high blood pressure.

### Is it safe to drink zobo every day?

For most healthy adults, a glass of lightly sweetened zobo a day is fine. Be careful if you take blood pressure or other regular medicine, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or have diabetes, and ask your doctor first.

### Can a pregnant woman drink zobo?

Little is known about the safety of drinking a lot of hibiscus in pregnancy, so many health workers advise caution. Ask your doctor or midwife before drinking zobo regularly while pregnant.

### How long does zobo last in the fridge?

Home-made zobo with no preservatives should be kept cold and drunk within a day or two. Throw it away if it smells sour, tastes fizzy or looks cloudy. Never leave it out in the sun.

### What is zobo called in English?

Zobo is made from roselle, also called hibiscus or sorrel (Hibiscus sabdariffa). The drink is often called hibiscus tea or sorrel drink in English.
