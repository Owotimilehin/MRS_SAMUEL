There is hot, and then there is Lagos in February and March hot. The sun bounces off the tarmac on Ikorodu Road, the danfo has no AC, and the NEPA light has gone just as you need the fan. Your body loses water through sweat all day long. This guide covers what to drink, which juices actually help, how much is sensible, and when heat becomes a health problem.

## Why Lagos heat drains you

Lagos is not just hot; it is humid. When the air is full of moisture, sweat evaporates more slowly, so your body has to work harder to cool down. You keep sweating, and you keep losing water and some salts.

Add long commutes, outdoor work, crowded markets and power cuts, and it is easy to get through half a day without drinking enough.

The hottest stretch is usually the dry-season months before the rains, roughly February to April, though heat and humidity stay high most of the year.

## Water first, juice second

Let us be honest: the best drink for heat is clean water. It has no sugar, it is cheap, and your body absorbs it well.

The [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/nutrition-and-healthy-eating/in-depth/water/art-20044256) notes that most fluids, including juice, count towards your daily intake, and that water-rich foods help too. So juice has a place, especially when it helps you drink more because you enjoy it. But it should top up your water, not replace it.

Fruit juice also carries natural sugar, which the [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news/item/04-03-2015-who-calls-on-countries-to-reduce-sugars-intake-among-adults-and-children) counts as free sugar to limit. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/5-a-day/5-a-day-what-counts/) suggests about 150ml of fruit juice or smoothie a day. Vegetable-heavy juices are a way to enjoy more without as much sugar.

## What makes a juice good for hot weather?

Look for these qualities:

- **High water content.** Watermelon, cucumber, orange and pineapple are all very juicy fruits.
- **Not too sweet.** Very sweet drinks can feel heavy in the heat. Tart and light is more refreshing.
- **Some potassium.** Sweat carries salts out of your body. Many fruits and vegetables contain potassium, one of the minerals your body uses to balance fluids, according to the [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Potassium-Consumer/).
- **Served cold.** A cold drink simply feels better and is easier to sip steadily.

## How much should you drink in the heat?

There is no single number that fits everyone. Your needs depend on your size, how active you are and how much you sweat. A few practical rules:

- Sip water steadily through the day rather than gulping a lot at once.
- Check your urine: pale yellow is a good sign; dark yellow means drink more.
- Have juice with meals, as a treat or a pick-me-up, in a small glass.
- Drink more if you work outdoors, exercise or have been sick with vomiting or diarrhoea.
- Go easy on alcohol and very sugary drinks, which do not help in the heat.

If you have heart or kidney problems or have been told to limit fluids, follow your doctor's advice instead.

## Warning signs of heat exhaustion

Know these signs, for yourself, older relatives, children and anyone working outside. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) lists:

- Tiredness, dizziness or a headache
- Feeling sick or being sick
- Heavy sweating with pale, clammy skin (harder to spot on brown or black skin)
- Cramps in the arms, legs or stomach
- A raised temperature
- Being very thirsty or irritable

If someone has these signs, move them somewhere cool, give them plenty of water (an oral rehydration drink also helps replace lost salts), and cool their skin with water and a fan. The NHS says heat exhaustion does not usually need emergency help if the person cools down within 30 minutes. If they do not, or if they have hot skin that is not sweating, a very high temperature, fast breathing, confusion, a seizure or lose consciousness, this may be heatstroke. Get emergency medical help straight away.

Juice is not a treatment for heat exhaustion. Water and oral rehydration salts (ORS) are.

## Keeping juice cold in Lagos

Fresh cold-pressed juice has no preservatives, so heat is its enemy too.

- Get it into the fridge as soon as it arrives.
- Do not leave bottles in a hot car, even for an hour.
- Carry juice in a cool bag with an ice pack for a long day out.
- Plan around power cuts: a cooler box with ice keeps bottles cold through a NEPA outage.
- Shake before you pour, and drink within the time on the label.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best juice to drink in hot weather?

Water-rich, lightly sweet juices are best: watermelon, cucumber, lemon, orange and pineapple, served cold. Drink them as a top-up to plain water, not instead of it.

### Is juice better than water for hydration?

No. Water is the best everyday drink for hydration because it has no sugar. Juice counts towards your fluids and can help you drink more if you enjoy it, but keep portions small.

### Is watermelon juice good for heat?

Yes. Watermelon is very juicy and naturally sweet, so watermelon-based juice is a refreshing way to add fluid on a hot day. It still contains natural sugar, so enjoy it alongside water.

### What should I drink for heat exhaustion?

Move to a cool place and drink plenty of water. An oral rehydration drink can help replace lost salts. If the person does not feel better within 30 minutes, or becomes confused or stops sweating, get emergency medical help.

### How do I keep fresh juice cold in Lagos?

Refrigerate it as soon as it arrives, never leave it in a hot car, and use a cool bag or cooler box with ice for days out or power cuts. Drink it within the time on the label.
