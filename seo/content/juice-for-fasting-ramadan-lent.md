When fasting for Ramadan or Lent, juice works best as a small part of breaking the fast, not the whole meal. Start with water, then a small glass of fresh juice with dates or food, and sip water until suhoor. Avoid juice-only fasts without medical advice, and if you have diabetes or another condition, see a doctor first.

Lagos fasts together. During Ramadan, families gather for iftar from Agege to Lekki, and during Lent many Christians fast until noon or 6pm, some following a Daniel fast of fruits and vegetables. In both cases, the question is the same: how do you stay hydrated and keep your energy up with long hours, heat and traffic? This guide is about drinks, especially juice, and how to use them sensibly.

We are a juice company, not doctors. This guide is general information. If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or take regular medicine, ask your doctor before you fast.

## How fasting affects your fluids

During a dawn-to-sunset fast, you take in no food or water for many hours. In Lagos heat, you also keep losing water through sweat. That is why the hours between breaking the fast and the next dawn matter so much: it is your only window to rehydrate.

Christian fasts vary more. Some people skip food until noon but drink water; others take nothing until 6pm; others follow a partial fast such as a Daniel fast. The principles below still apply, adjusted to your fast.

## Breaking the fast: water first, then a small juice

Many Muslims break the fast with dates and water, following tradition. That is also a sensible start nutritionally: water rehydrates, and dates give a quick, gentle lift of natural sugar.

After that, a small glass of fresh juice can:

- Add fluid in a form that is pleasant to drink when you are thirsty
- Bring some vitamin C and potassium
- Give you a little quick energy before the main meal

Keep the glass small, around 150ml. A big jug of juice on an empty stomach is a lot of sugar at once, and many people find it sits badly. The [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news/item/04-03-2015-who-calls-on-countries-to-reduce-sugars-intake-among-adults-and-children) counts juice sugars as free sugars to limit, even in a fresh juice with nothing added.

Then eat a proper meal, slowly.

## Suhoor and sahur: what to drink before dawn

Suhoor, the pre-dawn meal, is about lasting through the day. Diabetes UK's [Ramadan guidance](https://www.diabetes.org.uk/ramadan) advises drinking enough sugar-free, decaffeinated fluids before the fast begins, and rehydrating at iftar with sugar-free drinks, with water as the best choice. That advice is written for people with diabetes, but it is a sensible rule for everyone.

For drinks at suhoor:

- **Water first.** Sip it steadily from iftar to suhoor rather than drinking a lot at once just before dawn.
- **Something filling, not just sugary.** A thick, creamy drink with food lasts better than a quick sweet juice. [Pinecado Bliss](/juices/pinecado) (avocado and pineapple) is more filling than a thin juice. Pap, kunu or oats with milk are traditional choices too; see our [guide to kunu zaki and other types of kunu](/blog/kunu-zaki-types-of-kunu).
- **Easy on very salty food.** Salt makes you thirstier through the day.
- **Easy on lots of tea and coffee.** Diabetes UK suggests decaffeinated drinks before the fast, and strong caffeine at suhoor can also affect sleep.

## What about juice-only fasts and juice cleanses?

Some people want to do a "juice fast" during Lent, or replace meals with juice. We do not recommend a juice-only fast without medical advice.

- Juice lacks the protein and fat that keep you full and help keep your blood sugar steadier.
- Most of the fibre of the whole fruit is removed in juicing.
- Your liver and kidneys already handle "detox". Read [what actually goes into a detox juice](/blog/what-actually-goes-into-detox) for the honest story.

If you are doing a Daniel fast or a fruit-and-vegetable fast, aim to eat whole fruits, vegetables, beans and grains alongside any juice, not juice alone.

## Who should talk to a doctor before fasting

Religious rules usually excuse people who are sick, pregnant, breastfeeding, travelling or elderly from fasting. Medical advice is similar. Speak to a doctor before you fast if you:

- **Have diabetes**, especially if you use insulin or tablets that can cause low blood sugar. Diabetes UK advises speaking to your healthcare team before Ramadan, as your medicine may need adjusting, and breaking the fast if your blood sugar goes too low or above the level your team sets.
- **Have kidney disease, heart disease or high blood pressure.**
- **Are pregnant or breastfeeding.**
- **Take regular medicine** that must be taken at set times or with food.
- **Have a history of eating disorders.**

If you have diabetes and do fast, sweet juices at iftar can push your blood sugar up quickly. Ask your doctor or dietitian how juice fits your plan.

## Warning signs while fasting

Stop fasting and seek help if you feel:

- Dizzy, faint or confused
- Shaky, sweaty and weak (possible low blood sugar, especially in people with diabetes)
- Chest pain or a racing heart
- Very little or very dark urine with a severe headache

In Lagos heat, these signs can come on faster than you expect. Keep water close for when you break the fast, and do not ignore your body.

## Practical tips for fasting in Lagos

- **Plan your juice like you plan your meal.** Fresh juice has no preservatives, so order or make it close to when you will drink it, and keep it cold. See [Lagos traffic and the 48-hour shelf life](/blog/lagos-traffic-and-48-hour-shelf-life).
- **Avoid the hottest part of the day outdoors** where you can.
- **Keep portions small at iftar.** Eat slowly; a heavy meal fast can leave you sluggish for tarawih or evening service.
- **Share.** A tray of small juice bottles at iftar or after a church fast goes further than one big jug.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best drink to break a fast?

Water is the best first drink. Many Muslims break the fast with water and dates. After that, a small glass of fresh juice with food can add fluid, vitamin C and a little energy.

### Is juice good for iftar?

A small glass of fresh juice at iftar is fine for most healthy adults. Keep it to about 150ml, have it with dates or food, and drink plenty of water before suhoor. Avoid soft drinks and very sugary packaged juices.

### What should I drink at suhoor to avoid thirst?

Sip water steadily between iftar and suhoor, eat a balanced, filling suhoor, and go easy on salty foods. A filling drink like a creamy smoothie can help more than a quick sweet juice.

### Can I do a juice fast during Lent?

We do not recommend a juice-only fast without medical advice. Juice lacks protein, fat and much of the fruit's fibre. If you are doing a Daniel fast, eat whole fruits, vegetables, beans and grains alongside any juice.

### Can people with diabetes drink juice when breaking the fast?

Juice can raise blood sugar quickly, especially on an empty stomach. If you have diabetes, speak to your doctor or diabetes team before fasting and before adding juice to iftar.
