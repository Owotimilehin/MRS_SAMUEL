A ginger shot is a small, strong drink (usually 30 to 60ml) of fresh ginger juice, often with lemon, turmeric or pineapple. Research suggests ginger may ease nausea, especially in pregnancy, and it has a warming, wake-up effect many people enjoy. Evidence for detox, weight loss or immunity claims is weak. It is not a cure for anything.

Ginger shots have gone from health-shop novelty to something you see in Lagos office fridges and gym bags. They are cheap to make, quick to drink and very hot. Here is what a ginger shot actually does, what the science says, who should avoid it, and how to make a good one at home.

## What is a ginger shot?

A ginger shot is concentrated fresh ginger juice served in a small glass. Because it is so strong, people take it in one go, a bit like a shot at a bar, which is where the name comes from.

Common additions:

- **Lemon or lime** for vitamin C and a sharp taste
- **Turmeric** for colour and its compound curcumin
- **Pineapple or orange** to soften the heat
- **A pinch of black pepper** with turmeric
- **Honey**, though that adds sugar
- **Cayenne** for people who want more fire

Nigerian kitchens have used ginger for generations, in zobo, kunu, pepper soup and tea. A ginger shot is just the most concentrated way to take it.

## What is in ginger?

Fresh ginger root contains **gingerols**, the compounds that give it heat. When ginger is dried or cooked, some gingerols turn into **shogaols**, which are even more pungent. These compounds have been studied for their effects on nausea and inflammation, mostly in supplement form.

Ginger itself has very few calories and little sugar. Most of the sugar in a ginger shot comes from whatever fruit juice or honey is added.

## What the research says about ginger

The [NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH)](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ginger) gives a balanced summary:

- **Nausea in pregnancy:** research suggests ginger may be helpful.
- **Motion sickness:** most studies have not shown it helps.
- **Nausea after surgery or from chemotherapy:** it is uncertain whether ginger helps.
- **Safety:** ginger by mouth has been used safely in many studies, but it can cause stomach discomfort, heartburn, diarrhoea and mouth or throat irritation.

Note that most of these studies used ginger supplements in measured doses, not juice. A shot gives you an unmeasured amount.

## 5 possible benefits of ginger shots

**1. It may settle mild nausea**

This is ginger's best-supported use. A small amount of ginger can help some people with queasiness. If you are pregnant, ask your doctor or midwife before using ginger regularly.

**2. It may help digestion feel easier**

Ginger has a long traditional use for an unsettled stomach after a heavy meal. Human evidence for general digestion is limited, so think of it as a gentle aid.

**3. A caffeine-free wake-up**

The burn of a ginger shot gets your attention fast. Many people use it as a morning ritual in place of a second coffee. That is a real, if simple, benefit.

**4. Vitamin C from the citrus**

If your shot includes lemon, lime or orange, you get some vitamin C. The [NIH vitamin C fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-Consumer/) notes it supports normal immune function and helps you absorb iron. A shot is small, so the amount depends on how much citrus is in it.

**5. Turmeric, if you add it**

Turmeric is popular in shots. The [NCCIH turmeric page](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric) says we do not yet know enough to conclude that turmeric or its compound curcumin is beneficial for any health purpose. The most promising early research is on osteoarthritis. Black pepper (piperine) helps the body absorb curcumin, which is why recipes pair them. Enjoy turmeric for flavour and colour, and avoid high-strength curcumin supplements unless your doctor agrees, as NCCIH notes some highly absorbable forms may harm the liver.

## Claims to be careful with

You will see ginger shots sold as immune boosters, detoxes, fat burners and cold cures.

- **Immunity:** no single food or drink "boosts" your immune system. Sleep, a varied diet, vaccines and handwashing matter much more.
- **Detox:** your liver and kidneys do that job. See [what actually goes into a detox juice](/blog/what-actually-goes-into-detox).
- **Weight loss:** there is no good evidence that ginger shots cause meaningful weight loss.
- **Colds:** a warm ginger drink can feel soothing on a sore throat, but it does not cure a cold. Our [pineapple and ginger juice guide](/blog/pineapple-ginger-immunity) covers the evidence on colds.

## Side effects and who should avoid ginger shots

Concentrated ginger is strong. Common complaints are heartburn, a burning mouth or throat, stomach upset and loose stools.

Be careful, and talk to your doctor first, if you:

- **Take blood thinners or other regular medicine.** NCCIH advises talking with your health care provider before using ginger if you take any medicine, as herbs and medicines can interact.
- **Are pregnant or breastfeeding.** Ginger may help pregnancy nausea, but check the amount with your doctor or midwife. Little is known about safety while breastfeeding.
- **Have acid reflux, ulcers or a sensitive stomach.** A shot on an empty stomach is the most likely to cause trouble.
- **Have gallstones or a planned surgery.** Ask your doctor.
- **Have diabetes** and your shot includes juice or honey.

Children should not be given strong ginger shots.

## When to take a ginger shot

There is no proven best time. Many people take one in the morning. If it upsets your stomach, take it with or straight after breakfast. One small shot a day is plenty for most adults.

## How to make a ginger shot at home

This makes about four shots.

1. Wash a piece of fresh ginger about the size of your palm. No need to peel if it is clean and young.
2. Chop roughly and blend with the juice of two lemons and 100ml of fresh pineapple or orange juice.
3. Add a small piece of fresh turmeric and a pinch of black pepper, if you like.
4. Strain through a fine sieve or clean cloth, pressing hard.
5. Pour into a clean, airtight bottle and refrigerate.
6. Shake and drink one small shot at a time, within a couple of days.

Start with less ginger than you think. Ginger in Nigerian markets, much of it from Southern Kaduna, is famously hot.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a ginger shot do?

A ginger shot gives you a concentrated dose of fresh ginger, often with lemon or turmeric. Research suggests ginger may ease nausea, especially in pregnancy. Many people also enjoy it as a warming wake-up. It does not detox your body or cure illness.

### Is it good to take a ginger shot every day?

For most healthy adults, one small shot a day is usually fine. Stop if it causes heartburn or stomach upset. Talk to your doctor first if you take regular medicine, are pregnant or have reflux.

### When is the best time to take a ginger shot?

There is no proven best time. Many people take one in the morning. If it upsets your stomach, take it with food rather than on an empty stomach.

### What are the side effects of ginger shots?

Common side effects are heartburn, a burning mouth or throat, stomach discomfort and diarrhoea. They are more likely with large shots or on an empty stomach.

### Do ginger shots help with weight loss?

There is no good evidence that ginger shots cause meaningful weight loss. A balanced diet and regular activity matter far more.

### Can I make a ginger shot without a juicer?

Yes. Blend fresh ginger with lemon juice and a little pineapple or orange juice, then strain through a fine sieve or clean cloth. Keep it in the fridge and drink within a couple of days.
