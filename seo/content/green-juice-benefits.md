Green juice is a juice made mostly from green vegetables such as cucumber, celery, spinach and kale, with a little fruit for taste. It gives you fluid, vitamins and plant compounds with less fruit sugar than most fruit juices. It is a useful extra, but it does not detox your body or replace eating whole vegetables.

A few years ago you only saw green juice on foreign wellness pages. Now people in Lekki, Yaba and Ikeja ask for it by name. Some expect a miracle in a bottle. Others think it tastes like grass. The truth sits in the middle. Here is what is in a green juice, what research suggests it can do, where the hype goes too far, and who should check with a doctor first.

## What is in a green juice?

A green juice leans on vegetables, not fruit. A typical bottle has:

- **Cucumber**, which is mostly water and gives the juice a light body.
- **Leafy greens** such as spinach, kale or Nigerian ugu (fluted pumpkin leaf), which bring colour, folate and vitamin K.
- **Celery**, for a fresh, slightly salty taste.
- **A little fruit**, such as apple, pineapple or lemon, so it is pleasant to drink.

Because vegetables carry less sugar than sweet fruits like pineapple or orange, a vegetable-led juice usually carries less sugar than a fruit-only juice. The exact amount depends on the recipe.

## 6 things green juice may do for you

Here is what the evidence supports, and how strong it is.

**1. It helps you get more vegetables into your day**

Most people do not eat enough vegetables. The [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet) notes that many people now eat too few fruits and vegetables and too little fibre. Diets rich in vegetables are linked with lower risk of heart disease and stroke, as the [Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/what-should-you-eat/vegetables-and-fruits/) explains. A green juice is an easy way to add some vegetables if you struggle to eat them. It is an extra, not a swap for your efo riro.

**2. It gives you vitamins and minerals from leafy greens**

Spinach and kale are good sources of vitamin K, which your body uses for blood clotting and healthy bones, according to the [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-Consumer/). Leafy greens also bring folate and other plant compounds. Pressing keeps most vitamins and minerals in the liquid, as [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/nutrition-and-healthy-eating/expert-answers/juicing/faq-20058020) notes.

**3. It keeps you hydrated**

Cucumber and celery are mostly water. In Lagos heat, a cold glass counts towards your daily fluid. Plain water should still be your main drink, and the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/food-guidelines-and-food-labels/water-drinks-nutrition/) advises keeping fruit juice and smoothies to one small glass (150ml) a day, with a meal.

**4. It can replace a soft drink**

This is where green juice earns its place. The [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/healthy-drinks/) advises avoiding sugary drinks like soda and limiting juice to one small glass a day. If your afternoon habit is a chilled soft drink, a small glass of vegetable-led juice is a better swap. It is less sweet, so many people find they slowly lose the taste for very sugary drinks.

**5. It may be easier on blood sugar than sweet fruit juice**

With less sweet fruit in the bottle, a green juice is likely to carry less sugar than an orange or pineapple juice of the same size. But "less" is not "none". Juice still lacks most of the fibre that slows sugar down. If you have diabetes, read our [guide to juice and diabetes](/blog/juice-and-diabetes) before you buy.

**6. It is a habit that can lead to others**

Many of our customers say a morning green juice nudges them towards better choices for the rest of the day. That is not a medical effect, and we cannot promise it. But small, steady habits are how most healthy changes happen.

## What green juice does not do

Plenty of sellers make big promises. Here is where the evidence stops.

- **It does not "detox" you.** Your liver and kidneys clear waste from your body every day. The [NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH)](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/detoxes-and-cleanses-what-you-need-to-know) says there is no compelling research to support "detox" diets for removing toxins or for long-term weight loss.
- **It does not beat whole vegetables.** [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/nutrition-and-healthy-eating/expert-answers/juicing/faq-20058020) says there is little scientific evidence that juice is healthier than eating the whole fruit or vegetable, mainly because juicing removes most of the fibre.
- **It does not burn fat.** Any weight change comes from your overall diet and activity, not from one drink.
- **It does not cure, treat or prevent any disease.** Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling, not informing.

## Who should be careful with green juice

Green juice is food, but some people need to think before drinking it daily.

- **People on blood thinners such as warfarin.** Leafy greens are rich in vitamin K, which can affect how warfarin works. The [NIH Office of Dietary Supplements](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-Consumer/) advises people on warfarin to keep their vitamin K intake steady from day to day. Talk to your doctor before adding green juice.
- **People who have had kidney stones.** Spinach, other leafy greens and beets are high in oxalate. [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/detoxes-and-cleanses-what-you-need-to-know) notes that people prone to kidney stones should limit high-oxalate foods, and the [NIDDK](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/kidney-stones/eating-diet-nutrition) recommends getting diet advice based on your stone type.
- **People with kidney disease.** Some vegetables are high in potassium, which some kidney patients must limit. Ask your kidney doctor first.
- **Pregnant women, children and anyone with a weak immune system.** Fresh juice is not pasteurised. [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/detoxes-and-cleanses-what-you-need-to-know) warns that unpasteurised juices can make people sick, and the risk is higher in these groups. Ask your doctor or midwife.
- **People with diabetes.** Even green juice usually contains some fruit. Talk to your doctor or dietitian about portions.

If you have any medical condition or take regular medicine, talk to your doctor before making green juice a daily habit.

## How to drink green juice well

- **Keep the portion small.** About 150ml, the size of a small glass, is plenty for most adults.
- **Have it with food.** A glass alongside eggs, oats or moi moi is easier on your stomach and your teeth than sipping it alone.
- **Keep eating whole vegetables.** Juice is the extra. Your soups, salads and stews still bring the fibre.
- **Drink it very cold.** Cold softens the earthy taste. Shake the bottle first, as natural juice settles.
- **Start gentle.** If you are new to green juice, a lighter, less leafy blend is easier to like.

## Fresh green juice vs packaged "green" drinks

Some supermarket "green" drinks are mostly apple juice from concentrate, coloured green with a small amount of vegetable. Read the label. Ingredients are listed from most to least, so if apple or sugar comes first, it is a fruit drink with a green label.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the benefits of green juice?

Green juice helps you get more vegetables into your day, gives you vitamins such as vitamin K and folate, keeps you hydrated and makes a better swap for soft drinks. It usually has less sugar than fruit-only juice. It does not detox your body or cure any disease.

### Is it good to drink green juice every day?

For most healthy adults, a small glass a day is fine as part of a balanced diet. Keep eating whole vegetables too. If you take warfarin, have had kidney stones, have kidney disease or diabetes, or are pregnant, talk to your doctor first.

### Does green juice help you lose weight?

Not on its own. Swapping a daily soft drink for a small vegetable-led juice can cut sugar, which may help as part of an overall plan. No juice burns fat, and research does not support "detox" diets for long-term weight loss.

### Is green juice better than eating vegetables?

No. Juicing removes most of the fibre, which keeps you full, feeds your gut bacteria and slows how sugar enters your blood. Mayo Clinic says there is little evidence juice is healthier than whole produce. Think of green juice as an extra.

### When is the best time to drink green juice?

There is no strong evidence for a best time. Many people like it in the morning with breakfast. Drinking it with a meal is kinder to your teeth and stomach than sipping it alone through the day.

### What does green juice taste like?

Fresh, light and a little earthy, with a gently sweet finish from the apple or pineapple. The first sip is the strangest. Drink it very cold and give it a few sips before you decide.
