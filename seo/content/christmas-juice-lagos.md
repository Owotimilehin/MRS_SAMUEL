December in Lagos is a season of its own. The city fills up, the parties stack up, and the heat does not take a holiday. This guide helps you plan the drinks side of it: what to serve, how much to buy, what to give as gifts and how to keep it all fresh.

## What Christmas in Lagos really looks like

If you live here, you know the pattern. From early December the owambes start. Church harvests, end-of-year office parties, weddings that were waiting for "when everybody is home", and the long run of Detty December concerts and beach days.

Then there are the family visits. Aunties arrive from the village, cousins fly in from abroad, and every house becomes a small restaurant. Christmas Day itself is rice, chicken, small chops and a full table of guests who keep coming until night.

And through it all, the heat. December brings harmattan haze in some years, but the afternoons are still hot and the traffic is still the traffic. Whatever you serve has to be cold, and it has to stay cold.

## Alcohol-free drinks for every guest

At most Lagos Christmas gatherings, a good share of your guests will not drink alcohol. Think about who is actually at the party:

- **Children**, who will drink anything sweet and colourful if you let them.
- **Elders and church members**, many of whom do not drink at all.
- **Drivers**, who have to get everyone home safely through Third Mainland Bridge traffic.
- **People who simply choose not to drink**, for faith, health or personal reasons.
- **Pregnant and breastfeeding guests**, who need something better than plain water.

Too often, these guests get a warm can of soft drink while the "real" drinks go to everyone else. A row of chilled juice bottles changes that. It looks generous on the table, and it tells every guest they were planned for.

If a guest has diabetes or another health condition, they should follow their own doctor's advice on fruit juice, as with any sweet drink.

## How many bottles of juice per guest?

This is the question everyone asks when planning a Christmas party. There is no perfect number, because it depends on the heat, the food and how long people stay. But here is a simple rule of thumb.

**A guide, not a rule:**

1. **Short visit (1 to 2 hours):** about one 330ml bottle per guest.
2. **Party or owambe (3 hours or more):** about two 330ml bottles per guest, or one 650ml bottle per guest.
3. **Hot afternoon or outdoor event:** add around 20 percent on top.
4. **Lots of children:** plan for them to drink as much as the adults.

So for a Christmas lunch of 20 people that runs into the evening, you might plan around 40 small bottles, or 20 large ones. If you also serve water, soft drinks or other drinks, you can reduce the juice a little.

The small 330ml bottles work best for parties, because each guest gets their own sealed bottle and nothing is wasted. The 650ml bottles are better for family tables, where people pour into glasses and share.

There is **no maximum order**, and the minimum is just one bottle, so you can order for a quiet family dinner or for a hall full of guests.

## Juice for Christmas parties and events

For a wedding, end-of-year party, carol service, birthday or a big family owambe, see our [events page](/events-juice-lagos). Juice works well at events because the bottles are ready to serve: no mixing, no ice buckets of punch going warm, no one standing behind a table pouring all night.

A few tips that help on the day:

- **Put out a mix of flavours.** Some guests want something light, others want something rich or with a ginger kick.
- **Label the table.** A small card with each juice name and its fruits helps guests with allergies and picky children.
- **Keep the back-up stock in the fridge**, not on the table. Bring bottles out in batches.

## Keeping juice fresh and cold in December heat

Cold-pressed juice is a fresh product. It has no preservatives, so it needs to be treated like fresh food, not like a soft drink that can sit in a hot car for a week.

Here is how to keep it at its best:

- **Refrigerate as soon as it arrives.** Do not leave the delivery on the counter while you cook.
- **Plan fridge space before the party.** Christmas fridges are full of chicken, stew and cake. Clear a shelf for drinks.
- **Use cool boxes with ice for outdoor events**, beach trips and long drives to the village.
- **Do not leave bottles in a parked car.** A car in the Lagos sun gets very hot very fast.
- **Drink by the date on the bottle.**
