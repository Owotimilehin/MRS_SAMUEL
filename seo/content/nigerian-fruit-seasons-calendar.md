In Nigeria, the dry season (roughly November to March) brings oranges, watermelon, agbalumo and, towards its end, mangoes and cashew fruit. The rainy season (roughly April to October) brings avocado, ube, corn, guava and more pineapple. Bananas, plantain, pawpaw, coconut and lemons are around most of the year. Exact timing varies by region and year.

Ask any Lagos market woman and she will tell you: fruit has its time. Agbalumo in January tastes nothing like a stray one in July. Mangoes in April are cheap and sweet; in October they are scarce and dear. Knowing the seasons helps you eat better, spend less and enjoy fruit at its best.

A note before you start: this calendar is a practical guide, not a scientific record. Nigeria is large, with very different climates from the coast to the Sahel, and seasons shift with the rains each year. Much of Lagos's produce travels from other states, so a fruit may arrive in Lagos weeks before or after its peak where it is grown. When in doubt, ask your trader what is fresh this week.

## Nigeria's two main seasons

- **Dry season, roughly November to March.** Includes the harmattan, when dry, dusty winds blow from the Sahara, usually strongest from December to February.
- **Rainy season, roughly April to October.** In the South, the rains often ease for a short "August break" before returning in September and October.

Many fruits follow these seasons closely. Others, like banana, are harvested all year.

## Month-by-month fruit calendar

**January**

- Agbalumo (African star apple, udara) at its peak
- Oranges and tangerines plentiful
- Watermelon from the North in good supply
- Pineapple available
- Carrots and cucumbers plentiful
- Strawberries from the Jos Plateau in some markets

**February**

- Agbalumo still everywhere
- Oranges still good, starting to slow
- Watermelon plentiful
- First early mangoes in some markets
- Cashew fruit starting

**March**

- Mango season begins in earnest
- Cashew fruit in season
- Watermelon still good
- Agbalumo starting to fade
- Pineapple building up

**April**

- Mango at or near its peak, cheap and sweet
- Cashew fruit
- Pineapple increasing
- Early avocado (pear) in some markets
- First rains begin in the South

**May**

- Mango still good, but tailing off by the end of the month
- Pineapple plentiful
- Avocado coming in
- Ube (African pear) beginning
- Fresh corn starting

**June**

- Pineapple plentiful
- Avocado plentiful
- Ube in season
- Fresh corn: the corn-and-ube combination on every roadside
- Guava starting

**July**

- Avocado at or near its peak
- Ube and corn
- Guava in season
- Pineapple good
- Soursop showing up in some markets

**August**

- Avocado still plentiful
- Guava
- Ube and corn continuing
- Fresh tigernut appearing in markets
- Soursop in some markets

**September**

- Avocado starting to slow
- Guava
- Fresh tigernut in good supply
- Oranges beginning to return in some markets
- Ube finishing

**October**

- Oranges returning
- Guava finishing
- Fresh tigernut
- Pawpaw, banana and plantain steady as always
- Late avocado

**November**

- Oranges plentiful
- Watermelon coming back from the North
- Fresh zobo (hibiscus) harvest in the North, though dried zobo is sold all year
- Fresh tigernut still around
- Carrots and cucumbers improving

**December**

- Oranges and tangerines plentiful, a festive favourite
- Agbalumo arriving
- Watermelon plentiful
- Pineapple available
- Fresh ginger harvest in the North begins; dried ginger is sold all year

## Fruits you can find most of the year

These are grown in many places and harvested in waves, so Lagos markets rarely run out:

- **Banana and plantain**
- **Pawpaw (papaya)**
- **Coconut**
- **Lemons and limes**
- **Pineapple** (with a bigger supply in the middle of the year)
- **Carrots, cucumbers and leafy greens** (better and cheaper in the dry season)
- **Dried items:** dried zobo, dried ginger and dried tigernut store well, so they are sold year-round

Imported fruits such as apples, grapes and kiwi are available all year in supermarkets and big markets, but their price moves with the naira and import costs.

## Why eat in season?

- **Better taste.** Fruit picked at its natural time is usually riper and sweeter.
- **Lower prices.** When a fruit floods the market, prices drop. April mangoes and January agbalumo are the classic examples.
- **Less waste.** Seasonal fruit has travelled less time and is less likely to spoil before you eat it. Read our story on [fighting fruit waste](/blog/more-than-juice-fighting-fruit-waste).
- **More variety.** Following the seasons keeps your diet interesting. The [Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/what-should-you-eat/vegetables-and-fruits/) recommends eating plenty of vegetables and fruits of many colours.

## Tips for buying fruit in Lagos markets

- **Mango:** smell the stem end. A sweet, fruity smell means it is ripe. Avoid black patches and very soft spots.
- **Pineapple:** a ripe one smells sweet at the base and a leaf pulls out with a gentle tug. Heavy for its size is good.
- **Watermelon:** knock it. A deep, hollow sound and a creamy yellow patch where it lay on the ground are good signs.
- **Orange:** heavy, firm and slightly soft when squeezed. Nigerian oranges are often green-skinned even when ripe, so do not judge by colour.
- **Avocado:** gives slightly when pressed. Buy a mix of ripe and hard so they do not all ripen at once.
- **Agbalumo:** smooth, bright skin and a little give. Avoid wrinkled ones.
- **Guava:** fragrant and slightly soft. Eat quickly, as it spoils fast in heat.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is mango season in Nigeria?

Mango season in Nigeria usually runs from around February or March to May, with the cheapest, sweetest mangoes often around April. Timing varies by region and year.

### When is agbalumo season?

Agbalumo (African star apple, udara) is a dry-season fruit, usually plentiful from about December to March, with January often the peak in Lagos.

### When is avocado (pear) season in Nigeria?

Avocado is mainly a rainy-season fruit, usually from around May to September, with the most plentiful supply in the middle of the year.

### What fruits are in season in Nigeria all year round?

Banana, plantain, pawpaw, coconut, lemons and limes are available most of the year. Pineapple is also around most months, with a bigger supply mid-year.

### When is orange season in Nigeria?

Oranges are most plentiful in the dry season, roughly October or November to February. Nigerian oranges are often green-skinned even when ripe.

### When is ube (African pear) season?

Ube is a rainy-season fruit, usually from around May to September, often eaten with roasted or boiled corn.
